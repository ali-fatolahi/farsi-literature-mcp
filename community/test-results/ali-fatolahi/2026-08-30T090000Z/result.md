## Three Persian classical poetry passages on separation and longing (فراق و دلتنگی)

**1. Hafez (حافظ)** — *Ghazal 297, Divan-e Hafez*

> زبانِ خامه ندارد سرِ بیان فِراق
> وگرنه شرح دهم با تو داستانِ فِراق
>
> دریغ مدت عمرم که بر امیدِ وصال
> به سر رسید و نیامد به سر زمانِ فِراق

**Ganjoor URL:** https://ganjoor.net/hafez/ghazal/sh297

*Why it matches:* This entire twelve-couplet ghazal is built around the redif (repeated end-word) "فراق" (separation) itself — Hafez says his pen has no words adequate to describe separation's pain, making it one of the most direct and sustained treatments of فراق in the whole Divan.

---

**2. Rumi / Mowlana (مولانا)** — *Masnavi-ye Ma'navi, Book One, opening ("Ney-nāme")*

> بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند
> از جدایی‌ها حکایت می‌کند
>
> کز نِیِستان تا مرا بُریده‌اند
> در نفیرم مرد و زن نالیده‌اند
>
> سینه خواهم شَرحه‌شَرحه از فراق
> تا بگویم شرحِ دردِ اشتیاق

**Ganjoor URL:** https://ganjoor.net/moulavi/masnavi/daftar1/sh1

*Why it matches:* The reed's lament for its severance from the reed-bed is the founding metaphor of the entire Masnavi for spiritual separation (فراق) and longing (اشتیاق) — arguably the single most famous opening on this theme in all of Persian literature.

Note: Ganjoor's text (based on the Konya manuscript) opens with "بشنو این نی" ("Listen to this reed"); many popular editions instead print "بشنو از نی" ("Listen from the reed"). Both variants exist in the manuscript tradition — I've quoted Ganjoor's displayed text exactly.

---

**3. Saadi (سعدی)** — *Ghazal 279, Ghazaliyat (Divan-e Saadi)*

> فراق را دلی از سنگ سخت‌تر باید
> مرا دلیست که با شوق بر نمی‌آید
>
> هنوز با همه بدعهدیت دعاگویم
> بیا و گر همه دشنام می‌دهی شاید

**Ganjoor URL:** https://ganjoor.net/saadi/divan/ghazals/sh279

*Why it matches:* The opening line states outright that enduring فراق (separation) requires a heart harder than stone — Saadi confesses his own heart isn't equal to that burden, making longing and heartache the ghazal's explicit subject from its first words.
